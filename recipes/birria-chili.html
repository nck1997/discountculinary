Birria Chili

A weeknight chili built on birria-spiced beef - because birria seasoning (guajillo, ancho, arbol, cumin, Mexican oregano, garlic, a vinegar tang) is already the chili flavor family. Deep, beefy, mildly spicy, protein-dense. Great as tacos and bowls, or Cincinnati-style over protein spaghetti.

Steps

  1. Warm olive oil in a pot and cook the onion until soft and lightly golden. Add garlic and cook 1 min.
  2. Stir in tomato paste and cook until darkened, about 2 min.
  3. Add the birria beef (and optional ground beef) and stir to combine and heat through.
  4. Add crushed tomatoes and a splash of bone broth. Season LIGHTLY - taste first, the beef already carries chile, cumin, and oregano - then nudge with smoked paprika or chipotle. Add a pinch of sugar to balance the acid.
  5. Simmer low 30-45 min until rich and slightly thickened. If it tastes sharp, simmer longer or add another pinch of sugar (same fix as the butter chicken).
  6. Stir in beans if using and warm through.
  7. Finish with lime, raw diced onion, and cilantro.
  8. Variation: cook the onion in avocado oil, darken the tomato paste, then brown the ground beef. Add the shredded birria, diced tomatoes, cannellini beans, bone broth and the chili seasoning packet, and simmer until thick. No sugar in this one.
  9. Chili sandwiches: spoon the thick chili onto a toasted roll and top with pickled hatch jalapeños and hot sauce.
Cook's notes

The birria beef is a head start, not a complication - it is the chili flavor family already. Two adjustments because of it: season lighter on top (the beef carries chile/cumin/oregano), and mind the acid (Rosa San Marzano crushed plus a pinch of sugar). Goes Cincinnati-style over Brami protein spaghetti with cheddar and raw onion - that is the chili pasta. Or as tacos/bowl with Mission fiber tortillas. Macros are approximate, estimated from logged portions (a 418g plain bowl ran ~533 cal / ~51g protein / ~8g fiber). Variation notes: the real-birria version was the better of the two, though not by much. It's higher in protein and a bit richer, and the cannellini beans add creaminess, but the birria gets a little lost behind the chili seasoning. Worth making when there's leftover birria in the freezer; not worth making birria for.
